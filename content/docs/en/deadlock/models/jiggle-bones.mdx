<LevelPill level="advanced" />

Jiggle bones add a small bounce to part of a model when the hero moves. Set up extra bones in Blender, then control their movement in ModelDoc.

## What you need

<Requirements>
  <Requirement icon="Blender" href="/docs/deadlock/tools/blender-add-ons">
    Blender and an exporter
  </Requirement>
  <Requirement icon="Wrench" href="/docs/deadlock/tools/csdk-12">

Finish your mesh, materials, and basic weights first. Save copies of your `.blend` and `.vmdl` before changing the skeleton.

<Callout type="info" title="Check the result on your game version">
  Reed's guide includes corrections through July 2026. This workflow has not

  finished mod before sharing it.
</Callout>

## Pick the movement you need

| Method       | Use it for                                        |
| ------------ | ------------------------------------------------- |
| Jiggle bones | A small part that bounces around one root         |
| Cloth chains | A tail, rope, or strand with several moving bones |
| Proxy cloth  | A wider surface, such as a skirt or coat          |

The [cloth guide](/docs/deadlock/models/cloth) covers the other two methods.

Keep the movement close to the original hero's outline. Leave the hero's hitboxes, camera settings, and existing bones unchanged.

<Steps>
<Step>

### Add the bones in Blender

1. Select the hero's armature.
2. Enter Edit Mode.
3. Add a base bone near the center of the part you want to move.
4. Parent it to the hero bone that part follows.
5. Add a child bone near the moving tip.
6. Parent the child to your new base bone.
7. Name them `jiggle_base` and `jiggle_tip`.

Use different names for each pair if you add more than one.

</Step>
<Step>

### Check the base bone's axes

1. Open the armature's **Object Data Properties**.
2. Expand **Viewport Display** and turn on **Axes**.
3. Adjust the new base bone's direction and roll so its local X-axis points toward the child.
4. Leave the hero's original bones alone.

Blender draws a bone along its Y-axis. Reed's setup needs the base bone's local X-axis to point toward the jiggle tip.

Check the axis lines, not only the bone's shape.

<Step>

### Paint the weights

1. Select the mesh and enter Weight Paint mode.
2. Keep the fixed edge weighted to its original hero bone.
3. Paint the moving area onto the two new bone groups.
4. Give the base group more weight than the tip group.
5. Blend the weights into the fixed area.
6. Normalize the weights.
7. Pose the bones and check for sharp bends or loose vertices.

Reed's example splits one area's weight across three bones:

| Bone                     | Example weight |
| ------------------------ | -------------- |
| Original supporting bone | 0.5            |
| New base bone            | 0.3            |
| New tip bone             | 0.2            |

These values describe one example, not every vertex. Keep more weight on the original bone where the mesh should stay still.

<Step>

### Load the updated skeleton

1. Export the weighted mesh and armature using your model's existing export workflow.
2. Open the `.vmdl` in ModelDoc.
3. Check that the skeleton contains both new bone names.
4. Reimport the skeleton if the names or positions are missing.
5. Check the original bone names and hierarchy against your saved copy.
6. Compile the model.

Reed's July correction uses the skeleton node to reimport an updated FBX. Updating the render mesh alone may leave old skeleton data.

Do not replace the hero's AnimGraph 2 skeleton with a custom compiled skeleton.

{/* NEEDS HANDS-ON TEST: confirm the skeleton reimport controls for DMX and FBX in CSDK 12. Check that new bones update without changing original attachments, hitboxes, or the AG2 references. */}

</Step>
<Step>

### Add the JiggleBone node

1. Open ModelDoc's **Add** menu and search for `JiggleBone`.
2. Add one node for your new bone pair.
3. Set **Jiggle Root Bone** to `jiggle_base`.
4. Set **Jiggle Type** to **Rigid**, as in Reed's example.
5. Place **Point 1** at the child bone's location in the model preview.
6. Check that the jiggle gizmo points toward the moving tip.
7. Turn on the cloth simulation preview.

The point coordinates depend on your bone positions. Do not copy another model's coordinates.

## Tune the movement

Start with the node's defaults. Change one setting at a time and preview a walk or turn.

## Compile and test

1. Save your `.vmdl`.
2. Compile the model.
3. Follow [Add AnimGraph 2 and compile](/docs/deadlock/models/hero-swap-compile) to finish the hero model.
4. Pack your mod and test it in a private session.
5. Walk, jump, and turn in both directions.
6. Check the fixed edge, the moving tip, and nearby clothing for clipping.

The simulation preview can misbehave even when the compiled mod moves correctly. Check in game before changing all your weights.

## Common problems

<Accordions>
<Accordion title="The movement points into the model">

Check the base bone's local X-axis. It should point toward the child. Fix the new bone in Blender, export again, and update the skeleton.

</Accordion>
<Accordion title="The mesh stretches after an export">

Check that ModelDoc has the latest bone positions. Then check that the mesh's vertex group names match those bones.

</Accordion>
<Accordion title="The newest jiggle bone jitters in the preview">

The thread reports an inconsistent ModelDoc preview bug. Check the compiled mod before treating it as a weight problem.

{/* NEEDS HANDS-ON TEST: reproduce the last-added JiggleBone preview issue in current CSDK 12. Do not recommend the thread's temporary dummy-node workaround without testing it. */}

</Accordion>
<Accordion title="The part swings too far">

Lower its angle limits. Adjust stiffness and damping in small steps. Check that too much mesh weight has not moved onto the tip.

</Accordion>
