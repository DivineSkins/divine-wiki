<LevelPill level="advanced" />

Make tails, hair, coats, and skirts move on your custom hero model. You set up cloth in Blender and ModelDoc while you build the model. Finish [Hero swap part 1](/docs/deadlock/models/hero-swap-blender) and [part 2](/docs/deadlock/models/hero-swap-modeldoc) first.

## What you need

- A hero model that already compiles in ModelDoc. See [Hero swap part 2: build the model in ModelDoc](/docs/deadlock/models/hero-swap-modeldoc).
- Blender with a DMX exporter, such as BlenderSource2Tools (Fuji fork). See [Blender and add-ons for Deadlock](/docs/deadlock/tools/blender-add-ons).
- For proxy cloth: a way to copy vertex groups between meshes. Blender's own Data Transfer works. Hoxdolum's proxy cloth video uses the paid Mesh Data Transfer add-on.

## Pick a Blender version

Sources disagree on the best Blender version for cloth:

- Pugzilla's March 2026 model swap video recommends Blender 4.2 for cloth.
- Hoxdolum's 2024 cloth video says newer Blender versions cap the collision radius map at 1.
- The current Fuji fork supports Blender 4.4 and newer. For Blender 4.1 to 4.3.2, its notes point to the older release `3.3.1_2c`.

Use Blender 4.2 with Fuji fork `3.3.1_2c` for cloth until newer versions are tested.

## Cloth chains or proxy cloth

<img
  src="/wiki-images/deadlock-models-cloth-1.webp"
  alt="Two cloth methods side by side. A cloth chain is a line of new bones, such as a tail, set up with one Cloth Chain node. Proxy cloth is a simple single-sided mesh with four painted maps that moves a detailed skirt or coat."
/>

- **Cloth chain:** a line of new bones that swings. Use it for tails, ropes, and single strands of hair.
- **Proxy cloth:** a simple, low-poly mesh that moves a detailed one. Use it for skirts, coats, and capes.

## Before you start

- Never move, rename, or delete the hero's own bones. Adding new bones for cloth is fine.
- Keep the cloth close to the hero's outline. A huge cape or a wide skirt changes the hero's size on screen, and size changes are off limits.
- Source 2 Viewer only roughly rebuilds cloth when it decompiles a model. Use the original model's cloth nodes as a reference, not as finished settings.
- Save a copy of your `.blend` file before each export.

## Add a cloth chain

Basilisken adds a cloth chain to a tail in this video, from 51:49.

<YouTube
  id="IL3ywMmK090"
  start={3109}
  title="Adding a cloth chain to a custom hero model by Basilisken"
/>

In Blender:

1. Select the hero armature and go to Edit Mode.
2. Add new bones in a line, starting from the hero bone the cloth hangs from. For a tail, the video starts at the pelvis.
3. Connect each bone to the one before it. The video names the last bone `end`.
4. Weight the cloth part of your mesh to the new bones. For a quick start, parent it with **With Automatic Weights**.
5. Export the DMX the same way as in [part 1](/docs/deadlock/models/hero-swap-blender).

In ModelDoc:

1. Load the new bones. In the video, the author deletes the **Skeleton** node and adds a new skeleton from the DMX, then compiles.
2. Add a **Cloth Chain** node. Pick the first bone of the chain.
3. Turn on the simulation preview. Then turn on **wobble preview**, so the chain keeps moving while you orbit the camera.
4. Set **Twist Relax** to 1, so the bones do not twist.
5. Raise **Goal Strength** to about 7 to make the chain less floppy. Then lower it bone by bone toward the tip.
6. Compile.

### Make the proxy

1. Add a new mesh at the world origin. For a skirt, the video starts from a cylinder with 32 vertices.
2. Switch to Wireframe view. Shape the mesh roughly over the cloth.
3. Keep it low-poly and single-sided: no thickness and no double faces.
4. Add a few edge loops where the cloth needs more control, such as the hem.
5. Keep the proxy as its own object.
6. Choose **Object > Apply > All Transforms**.
7. Add an **Armature** modifier that points at the hero armature.
8. Copy the vertex groups from the real cloth mesh onto the proxy. Use World space and Vertex Groups.
9. Pose the armature in Pose Mode. Check that the proxy follows.

### Paint the four maps

1. In the **Source Engine Export** panel, set DMX to **Binary 9** and **Model 22**. The float map options appear.
2. Add the four maps in the table below.
3. Paint each map in Weight Paint mode. The Gradient tool works well: Linear for a smooth fade, Radial for one spot.

| Map              | What it controls                         | How to paint it                                                   |
| ---------------- | ---------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Cloth enable     | Which parts move as cloth                | Red parts simulate. Blue parts stay fixed.                        |
| Goal strength    | How closely the cloth follows the motion | Works the other way: high values hold the cloth to the animation. |
| Collision radius | Size of the collision spheres            | Most at the bottom, near the legs. Little at the top.             |
| Ground collision | Contact with the ground                  | Leave it at one flat value.                                       |

### Copy the maps onto the real mesh

The maps must sit on the mesh players see. A donor copy moves them across.

<img
  src="/wiki-images/deadlock-models-cloth-2.webp"
  alt="Five steps: paint four maps on the proxy, duplicate it as a donor and keep only the maps, copy them onto the real skirt, export DMX with Binary 9 and Model 22, then add the proxy node, Add bones to render mesh, and Cloth Params in ModelDoc."
/>

1. Duplicate the proxy. If the copy moves, set its location back to zero. This copy is the donor.
2. On the donor, open **Data Properties > Vertex Groups**. Lock the four map groups.
3. Delete every group that is not locked.
4. Select the real cloth mesh. Copy the donor's vertex groups onto it, with World space and Vertex Groups.
5. Check that the four maps are on the real mesh. Then delete the donor.
6. If the exporter asks for a material, give the mesh one. If it asks for an action, create one in the Dope Sheet.
7. Export. You can ignore warnings about vertices.

### Set it up in ModelDoc

1. Add a cloth proxy mesh node. Point it at the proxy's DMX file.
2. On that node, run **add bones to render mesh**. Without this step, the proxy does not move the real mesh.
3. Add a **Cloth Params** node. Leave it at its defaults for now. It affects every cloth part on the model, including hair and chains.
4. Turn on the simulation preview and play a walk.

## Fix clipping and stretching

- **The cloth clips through the legs.** The collision spheres are too small. Paint more collision radius near the bottom.
- **The radius will not go above 1.** This is the Blender version limit from the 2024 video. That author repainted the map in Blender 2.93, with values up to 5. Move the proxy between Blender versions as a DMX file, never by copy and paste.
- **The cloth stretches.** Fix the proxy's weights first. Change Cloth Params last, in small steps.

## Test in ModelDoc and in game

1. Preview a walk and a turn in ModelDoc. Check every cloth part, not only the new one.
2. Compile.
3. Finish the model with [part 3](/docs/deadlock/models/hero-swap-attachments) and [part 4](/docs/deadlock/models/hero-swap-compile).
4. Pack and test in game. Walk, jump, and turn fast. Follow [Test your mod in game](/docs/deadlock/basics/test-your-mod).

## Common problems

- **The proxy does nothing.** Run **add bones to render mesh** on the proxy node.
- **A chain twists.** Set **Twist Relax** to 1.
- **A chain is too floppy.** Raise **Goal Strength**.
- **Hair broke after you changed Cloth Params.** Cloth Params affects the whole model. Undo the change and adjust weights instead.
- **ModelDoc crashes when you open the model.** Delete the `_cloth_` bones that came from the decompile.
- **Weights vanished after you moved a mesh between Blender versions.** Export DMX from one version and import it in the other.

Community knowledge on cloth is thin. One Deadlock video covers proxy cloth, and it is from 2024. Nobody has published cloth settings for each hero.
