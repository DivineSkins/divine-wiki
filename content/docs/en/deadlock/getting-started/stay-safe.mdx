Read this before you install or make your first mod. It shows which mods cross the line and how to avoid malware.

## What you need

- Nothing to install.
- An archive tool, such as 7-Zip, to look inside mod downloads.

## How mods work in a match

Mods are client-side. You see a skin on every player who uses that hero, enemies included. Other players see the normal game.

So a skin can give you an advantage by accident: a bright skin makes an enemy easier to spot.

## What Valve has said

Valve has published no mod policy for Deadlock, so nobody can promise what is allowed. Its update notes never mention player mods, and Valve lists mod support as "Unsupported" in the invite-only playtest. The choice to use mods is yours.

Valve has changed the game, though:

- Since March 2026, matchmaking refuses to start if anyone in your party runs the creator tools or has changed certain parts of `gameinfo.gi`.
- Since September 2026, the game ignores many visibility settings in `gameinfo.gi`, such as enemy outlines and glow.

The Steam Subscriber Agreement lets Valve close accounts that use mods for an unfair advantage. Reports from 2024 say a ban for unfair play also banned every player that account invited to the playtest.

## Mods you can make

This wiki covers mods that change how your game looks and sounds, plus packing, installing, and publishing them.

Before you share a mod, ask: does it show me anything the normal game hides? If yes, it crosses the line. Check your skin from the enemy's side too. A skin that glows or turns very dark is easier to spot.

## Mods we never cover

These mods give you an advantage over players without them. GameBanana's Deadlock rules ban many of them. This wiki will never explain, link, or show them.

| Type                   | Examples                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| ---------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Timers and trackers    | Camp or buff respawn timers, cooldown or soul trackers, enemy health numbers, ranks, missing-enemy alerts, announcer packs that call objectives                                                 |
| Louder enemy cues      | Louder or longer heavy melee, parry, ultimate, or footstep sounds                                                                                                                               |
| Visibility changes     | Glowing, bright, see-through, or very dark skins; skins you can see through veils or fog; removed outlines, particles, or smoke; brighter lighting; fog turned off; a wider view; scope removal |
| Size and game data     | Smaller, thinner, or taller hero models; hitbox changes; camera changes; gameplay data changes                                                                                                  |
| Config and code tricks | Console variable edits in `gameinfo.gi`, code injection, overlays, "mod menus"                                                                                                                  |

Size matters in Deadlock. A smaller or thinner hero blocks less of your screen, and a taller one can see over cover. Keep every skin the same size as the original.

## Keep your account in good standing

1. Edit only the `SearchPaths` block of `gameinfo.gi`. It is the only part a mod needs. Changes to other sections can block matchmaking for your whole party.
2. Never paste a "performance" or config `gameinfo.gi` file from the internet. These files change the blocked sections.
3. Close CSDK 12 and every other creator tool before you queue.
4. Remove custom map mods before a normal match. Custom maps and gameplay data mods do not work in Valve's matches.

If the game blocks you, you see this message:

```text
Unable to enter matchmaking while any party member is running Tools-mode, or has unsupported changes to the following sections of their 'gameinfo.gi': Engine2, MaterialSystem2, NetworkSystem, Particles, RenderSystem, SceneSystem, WorldRenderer
```

To fix it, close all creator tools. Run **Verify integrity of game files** in Steam, then redo only the `SearchPaths` edit, by hand or with your mod manager.

## Download safely

### Know what a real mod looks like

- A Deadlock mod is a `.vpk` file, usually inside a `.zip`, `.rar`, or `.7z` archive, often with a readme.
- A mod never needs an `.exe`, `.dll`, `.bat`, `.ps1`, or `.scr` file. If an archive has one, do not open it. Delete the download.
- GameBanana scans every upload with antivirus software. A file that passed shows a green **CLEAN** tag. This is a first check, not a guarantee.

### Watch out for fakes

- `deadlockmodmanager.com` is an unofficial lookalike, not the Deadlock Mod Manager site.
- GitHub repos named `deadlock-mod-manager-mods` are fakes. They copy the real name but hold no real code.
- Repos that promise a "mod menu", "ESP", or a "Deadlock 2026" toolkit are bait: tools for unfair play, or malware. Never download them.
- `steamdb.com` is not SteamDB. The real SteamDB is `steamdb.info`.

## Respect other creators

- Most Deadlock mods use the CC BY-NC-ND 4.0 license: give credit, never sell, and never share changed versions. Ask the author before you edit, reupload, or reuse their work.
- Credit everyone whose work you use. GameBanana holds back mods with missing credits.
- Never sell your mods. Valve allows only non-commercial use of content made with its tools. GameBanana allows paid early access for 30 days at most.
- Never upload AI-generated art, models, voices, or music to GameBanana's Deadlock section. Its rules ban them.

[Credits, licenses, and permissions](/docs/deadlock/publishing/credits-and-licenses) explains the details.
