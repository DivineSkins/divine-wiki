This page is your map to Deadlock modding. It explains what a mod is, what you can make, and which guide to read first. You do not need any tools yet.

## What a Deadlock mod is

A Deadlock mod is a file that ends in `.vpk`. It holds new versions of game files, such as a hero texture or a sound. Each new file sits at the same path as the game file it replaces.

The game loads mods from one folder: `game/citadel/addons/`. A mod manager sets this up for you. You can also do it by hand.

Mods are client-side. They change the game on your PC only. Other players see the normal game.

[How Deadlock mods work](/docs/deadlock/basics/how-mods-work) explains this in more detail.

## What you can make

This wiki covers mods that change how the game looks and sounds:

- Hero skins, recolors, and materials.
- Sounds for your own hero, voice lines, and music.
- Hero portraits, icons, item art, and menu art.
- Text, such as item names.

We never cover mods that give you an advantage. That includes timers, enemy info, louder enemy sounds, bright or see-through skins, and smaller models. [What's allowed and how to stay safe](/docs/deadlock/getting-started/stay-safe) lists them all and explains why.

## What you need

To play with mods:

- Deadlock on Steam, updated to the latest version.
- A Windows or Linux PC.
- [Deadlock Mod Manager](/docs/deadlock/getting-started/install-with-mod-manager), or a text editor to [install mods by hand](/docs/deadlock/getting-started/install-by-hand).

To make your own mods:

- A Windows PC. The main creator toolset runs on Windows only.
- Source 2 Viewer, to open and export game files.
- CSDK 12, a free, unofficial community toolset, to compile your files.
- An image editor, such as GIMP or Photoshop.
- An audio editor, such as Audacity.
- Blender, later, if you want to change 3D models.

[Set up your creator tools](/docs/deadlock/getting-started/creator-setup) walks you through each install.

## Frequently asked questions

<Accordions type="multiple">

<Accordion title="Is modding Deadlock allowed?">

Valve has not published a mod policy for Deadlock. The game is still an invite-only playtest, and Valve lists mod support as "Unsupported".

Many players use cosmetic mods. As of October 2026, no one has reported a confirmed ban for one. That is not a promise.

Read [What's allowed and how to stay safe](/docs/deadlock/getting-started/stay-safe) before you install anything.

</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Do other players see my mods?">

No. You see a skin on every copy of that hero in your game, enemies included. Nobody else sees it.

This is why a bright skin can give you an unfair advantage by accident.

</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Is there an official modding kit?">

No. Valve ships no modding tools for Deadlock. The community built its own toolset, CSDK 12, from Valve's Counter-Strike 2 Workshop Tools.

</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Do mods break after a game update?">

Often, yes. Updates replace the file that tells the game to load mods. A mod manager fixes this the next time you launch through it.

Big updates also break some mods, most of all HUD mods. Those stay broken until their creators update them.

[Install mods by hand](/docs/deadlock/getting-started/install-by-hand) shows what to do after an update.

</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Can I sell my mods?">

No. Valve's Steam Subscriber Agreement allows only non-commercial use of content made with its tools. GameBanana does not allow selling mods either.

The publishing guides will explain credits and licenses. {/* LINK LATER: /docs/deadlock/publishing/credits-and-licenses */}

</Accordion>

</Accordions>
