A weapon is the simplest whole model to swap in Wuthering Waves: one object, usually moved by one bone. This guide replaces a weapon's model, then says what is known about props, echoes and other objects. Written for WWMI Tools 1.8.0 on Blender 4.5 LTS, with WWMI 1.1.0.

## What you can swap

| Object                      | What is known                                                                                      |
| --------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Weapons                     | Import them **Per-Component**. Most use one vertex group, `0`. The steps below.                    |
| Props a Resonator holds     | Their own objects, like Brant's compass or Encore's puppet. Dump the Resonator while it holds one. |
| Echoes                      | WWMI Tools extracts them. **Merged** suits echoes with complex weights.                            |
| Open-world objects and NPCs | WWMI Tools 1.3.5 and newer extract them. NPCs share models, so a mod changes every copy.           |
| Gliders                     | Glider mods exist, but no tested steps for this game yet. Not covered here.                        |

A Resonator is the game's name for a playable character. Per-Component also avoids a known limit of the Merged skeleton: two copies of the same modded object on screen glitch.

## Get the weapon

1. Remove any mod for that weapon from your `Mods` folder and press `F10`.
2. Equip the weapon on a Resonator and open the weapon screen.
3. Make a frame dump and extract it, as [Make a frame dump](/docs/wuthering-waves/basics/frame-dump) shows.
4. Import the extracted folders one by one to find the weapon. Rename its folder to something readable.

[WWMI-Assets](https://github.com/SpectrumQT/WWMI-Assets) has ready weapons in `WeaponData`, such as `AugustaSword` or `LupaSpear`. It stops at game version 2.7, so use it for practice only.

## Replace a weapon model

<img
  src="/wiki-images/wuthering-waves-models-swap-weapons-and-objects-1.webp"
  alt="Six steps: dump the weapon from the weapon screen, import it Per-Component with your own model, line your model up over the original, weight it to group 0 at full weight, join it into the emptied original, and export Per-Component and test with F10"
/>

<Steps>
<Step>

### Import both models

1. Import the weapon's folder with **Import Object** and the **Per-Component** skeleton, as [Get a model into Blender](/docs/wuthering-waves/models/import-into-blender#merged-or-per-component) shows.
2. Check the weapon object's **Vertex Groups** list in the **Object Data** tab. Most weapons have only `0`.
3. Import your model with <MenuPath path="File > Import" /> as FBX, OBJ or glTF.
4. Remove what the game cannot use: extra objects, bones and shape keys. One object with one texture is easiest.

</Step>
<Step>

### Line it up

1. Move, rotate and scale your model with `G`, `R` and `S` over the original. Put the grip where the hand holds the original.
2. Keep the size close to the original, or it clips through the Resonator.
3. Leave the original's rotation and scale as they are. Export applies your model's rotation and scale for you.

</Step>
<Step>

### Weight it to one bone

1. Delete your model's own vertex groups.
2. Add a group named `0`, select everything in Edit Mode, set **Weight** to `1.000` and click **Assign**.
3. In Weight Paint mode, run **Weights** > **Normalize All**.

[Weight paint a mesh](/docs/wuthering-waves/models/weight-painting#give-a-rigid-piece-one-bone) shows these steps with images. If the original weapon has more groups with weight, copy its weights with Data Transfer instead, as that page shows.

</Step>
<Step>

### Match the names and colors

1. Name your UV maps exactly like the original's: `TEXCOORD.xy`, `TEXCOORD1.xy` and `TEXCOORD2.xy`.
2. Copy `COLOR` and `COLOR1` from the original weapon, as [UV maps, vertex colors, and outlines](/docs/wuthering-waves/models/uvs-and-vertex-colors) shows. They control the outline.

</Step>
<Step>

### Join it into the original

1. Select the original weapon object, press `Tab`, press `A`, then `X` > **Vertices**. The object is now empty but keeps its name and data.
2. In Object Mode, select your model, then `Shift+click` the empty original so it is active.
3. Press `Ctrl+J`. Your model now has the original's name, such as `Component 0`.

</Step>
<Step>

### Make the textures

1. Paint the diffuse for your model's UVs and save it as BC7 sRGB. Keep an alpha channel like the original's.
2. Fill the light map with one color picked from the original weapon's light map. Save it and the normal map as BC7 linear.
3. Give each file the name of the original texture it replaces. The `t=` part of the name is the hash the mod matches.

[Save DDS textures the right way](/docs/wuthering-waves/textures/save-dds-files) has the save settings.

{/* NEEDS HANDS-ON TEST: carried over from the shared XXMI workflow, check it on Wuthering Waves: what the diffuse alpha does on a weapon in 3.8, and whether a one-color light map picked from the original looks right */}

</Step>
<Step>

### Export and test

1. Export with the **Per-Component** skeleton and the weapon's folder as **Object Sources**, as [Export your mod from Blender](/docs/wuthering-waves/models/export-your-mod) shows.
2. Copy your textures over the exported ones in the mod's `Textures` folder.
3. In game, equip the weapon, switch to another Resonator, press `F10`, and switch back.
4. Check the weapon standing, attacking, and when it appears and disappears.

{/* SCREENSHOT: wuthering-waves-models-swap-weapons-and-objects-2.webp, a Resonator in the Wuthering Waves 3.8 weapon screen holding a replaced weapon after F10 */}

{/* NEEDS HANDS-ON TEST: a weapon replaced with these steps on Wuthering Waves 3.8, WWMI Tools 1.8.0 and WWMI 1.1.0: the join keeps the original's data and the weapon follows the hand */}

</Step>
</Steps>

## Who sees your weapon

A mod matches the weapon's model, not the Resonator who holds it. So every Resonator who equips that weapon will most likely show your mod. Say so in your mod's description.

No tested way exists yet to limit a weapon mod to one Resonator in Wuthering Waves.

{/* NEEDS HANDS-ON TEST: on Wuthering Waves 3.8, a weapon mod shows on every Resonator who equips the weapon */}

## Props, echoes and other objects

These use the same pipeline as a Resonator: dump, extract, import, edit, export. No step-by-step guide for them exists yet, so test each one.

- **Props.** Dump the Resonator while it holds the prop. Extraction gives you a second folder for the prop. Import that folder, not the Resonator's.
- **Echoes.** Pick **Merged** when you weight parts to other bones, **Per-Component** for simple edits and retextures. Some echoes have a simpler skeleton, which WWMI Tools supports since 1.3.5.
- **Open-world objects and NPCs.** Lower the game resolution before you dump. Busy scenes with many characters can make extraction fail. The frame dump page covers that case.

## Common problems

<Accordions type="multiple">
<Accordion title="The weapon looks broken or stretched" id="broken-weapon">

The export **Skeleton** does not match the import. Set it to **Per-Component**, the mode you imported with, and export again.

</Accordion>
<Accordion title="The weapon does not follow the hand" id="weapon-floats">

Some vertices have no weight, or weights from your old model. Remove all vertex groups, assign group `0` at `1.000` to everything, and run **Normalize All**.

</Accordion>
<Accordion title="The texture is blank or scrambled" id="blank-texture">

A UV map name does not match the original's, or the texture file has another name than the original. Check both, then export again.

</Accordion>
<Accordion title="Parts of the weapon are black" id="black-parts">

Some faces point inward. Turn on the **Face Orientation** overlay, select the red faces, and press `Alt+N` > **Flip**.

</Accordion>
</Accordions>

More symptoms are on [Model problems in game](/docs/wuthering-waves/errors/model-problems).
