Weights tell the game which bones move each vertex of a mesh. A new part with no weights, or bad ones, stays frozen or stretches into spikes when the character moves. This page shows how to copy weights from the original part, fix what is left, and clean the groups so the export works. Written for XXMI Tools 1.8.2 on Blender 4.5 LTS.

Before you start, duplicate the imported original part (`Shift+D`, then right-click to drop it in place) and hide the copy with `H`. That copy is your weight source. You will join your finished mesh into the original later, as [Export your mod from Blender](/docs/honkai-star-rail/models/export-your-mod) shows.

## How weights work in Honkai: Star Rail

Each vertex group on an imported part is one bone of the character. The group's name is the bone's number. A weight from 0 to 1 says how strongly that bone moves the vertex: blue is 0, red is 1.

<img
  src="/wiki-images/honkai-star-rail-models-weight-painting-1.webp"
  alt="A banana-shaped test mesh shown five times, once for each vertex group 0 to 4, red where that bone moves the mesh and blue where it does not"
/>

XXMI Tools does not read the group names on export. It writes each vertex's bone as the position of the group in the list. That gives five rules:

| Rule                                         | What happens if you break it                                                                             |
| -------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Groups are `0`, `1`, `2`... in order, no gap | A missing number shifts every bone after it. The mesh explodes in game.                                  |
| Keep every group the original part had       | Same as above. A group with no weight in it is fine.                                                     |
| No new bones                                 | The skeleton is fixed. Long hair and cloth must follow bones the part already has.                       |
| At most 4 weights per vertex                 | SRMI poses character parts with 4 bones per vertex. The exporter warns about more.                       |
| Every vertex of a moving part has weight     | The part does not follow the body. The exporter warns `Mesh(<name>) requires vertex groups to be posed`. |

Each Star Rail component, `Hair`, `Head` and `Body`, comes with its own group list. Always take the numbers from the part your mesh will join.

The video uses Blender 2.80. In Blender 4.5 the "2D falloff" option is **Falloff Shape** set to `Projected`. Its automatic weights make groups named after your own bones: the game cannot use those, so copy weights from the original instead.

## Copy weights with Data Transfer

This is the fastest way to weight clothing or any mesh that sits on the body.

<Steps>
<Step>

### Line the mesh up

1. Place your mesh where it should sit on the original part. The closer it fits, the better the copy.
2. Press `Ctrl+A` and choose **All Transforms**.

</Step>
<Step>

### Add the modifier

1. Select your mesh and open the **Modifiers** tab (the wrench) in the Properties editor.
2. Add a **Data Transfer** modifier.
3. Set **Source** to the hidden copy of the original part.
4. Keep the **Object Transform** button to the right of **Source** turned on (blue). It makes the copy use world positions.
5. Tick **Vertex Data** and pick **Vertex Groups**.
6. Change **Mapping** from `Nearest Vertex` to `Nearest Face Interpolated`. This avoids spiky weights.
7. Leave **Layer Selection** on `All Layers` and **Layer Mapping** on `By Name`.
8. Click **Generate Data Layers**. It adds the groups `0`, `1`, `2`... to your mesh. Without them the modifier copies nothing.

<img
  src="/wiki-images/honkai-star-rail-models-weight-painting-2.webp"
  alt="The Data Transfer modifier panel with Source set to an object named OriginalBow, Vertex Data ticked, Vertex Groups picked, Mapping still on Nearest Vertex, Layer Selection All Layers, Layer Mapping By Name, and the Generate Data Layers button"
/>

The picture comes from a weapon guide, so its source object is a bow. The panel is the same for any part.

{/* NEEDS HANDS-ON TEST: whether Generate Data Layers creates the groups in the original's order on Blender 4.5. The clean-up below sorts them either way. */}

</Step>
<Step>

### Apply it and check

1. Click the down arrow in the modifier's header and choose **Apply**. Or hover over the modifier and press `Ctrl+A`.
2. Press `Ctrl+Tab` to enter Weight Paint mode.
3. Click through a few groups in the **Vertex Groups** list. The colors should look like the original's in the same place.

</Step>
</Steps>

Data Transfer is fast but rough. Expect to fix spots where parts touch: arms against the body, between the legs, and fingers.

## Give a rigid piece one bone

A hairpin or a badge should not bend. Give all of it one group at full weight.

1. Find the bone. In Weight Paint mode on the original part, click through its groups until the area under your piece turns red. Note that number.
2. Select your piece, press `Tab` for Edit Mode, and press `A` to select everything.
3. In the **Object Data** tab (the green triangle), under **Vertex Groups**, click `+`. Double-click the new group and rename it to the bone's number, for example `34`.
4. Set **Weight** to `1.000` and click **Assign**.

The other numbers come back when you join the piece into the original part, or when you run **Fill gaps in Vertex Groups** in the clean-up below.

## Fix one area

### Copy one group again

Use this when one group came out wrong, for example the upper arm grabbed the body.

1. In Object Mode, click the original copy, then `Shift+click` your mesh so it is the active object.
2. In each object's **Vertex Groups** list, select the same group.
3. Press `Ctrl+Tab` for Weight Paint mode.
4. Open the **Weights** menu and choose **Transfer Weights**.
5. In the panel at the bottom left, set **Vertex Mapping** to `Nearest Face Interpolated`, the source and destination layers to `Active Layer`, and **Mix Mode** to `Replace`.
6. Open **Weights** again and choose **Normalize All**, with **Lock Active** off. Each vertex's weights now add up to 1.

<img

  alt="The Weights menu in Weight Paint mode with Normalize All highlighted and its tooltip, plus Smooth, Transfer Weights and Limit Total in the list"
/>

### Paint it by hand

1. In Weight Paint mode, pick the group to change.
2. Set the brush **Weight** (the value you paint) and **Strength** (1.0 replaces what is there).
3. Paint. To take weight away, set **Weight** to `0`.
4. Use the **Blur** or **Average** brush on hard edges between red and blue.
5. To paint the back side too, set **Falloff Shape** to `Projected` and untick **Front Faces Only**.
6. Finish with **Weights** > **Smooth**, then **Weights** > **Normalize All**.

Keep **Auto Normalize** on in the tool options while you paint with brushes. Turn it off when you use the **Gradient** tool.

## Test with an armature

Posing the mesh in Blender finds bad weights before you start the game. This part is optional.

<ToolCard
  name="AnimeGameArmatures"
  href="https://github.com/Seris0/AnimeGameArmatures"
  badge="Untested"
  icon={<Icon name="GitHub" />}
>
  Ready-made armatures, with a folder for Honkai: Star Rail characters. By
  Seris0.
</ToolCard>

1. Append your character's armature from its `.blend` file into your scene.
2. On your mesh, add an **Armature** modifier and set **Object** to that armature. Do not apply it.
3. Select the armature, press `Ctrl+Tab` for Pose Mode, and press `Alt+Z` for X-ray.
4. Rotate the arms, legs and head with `R`. Watch for parts that stretch or stay behind.
5. If a bone moves a part it should not, keep it posed. Switch to your mesh in Weight Paint mode and `Shift+right-click` the bad area: Blender lists the groups with weight there. Pick the wrong one and paint it to `0` until the mesh snaps back.
6. When you finish, select all bones in Pose Mode and press `Alt+G`, `Alt+R` and `Alt+S` to return to the rest pose. Turn the Armature modifier off before you export.

## Clean up before every export

Run this on the final object, after you join your mesh into the original part.

1. Press `N` in the 3D Viewport and open **XXMI Tools** > **XXMI Toolbox**.
2. Click **Merge shared name Vertex Groups**. It folds `12.001` into `12` and sorts the list.
3. On a new part only, click **Remove unused Vertex Groups**.
4. Click **Fill gaps in Vertex Groups**. Always run it after step 3, or every number after a removed group shifts.
5. Keep **Normalize weights to format** on in the export settings. It keeps 4 weights per vertex and normalizes them. To do it by hand, use **Weights** > **Limit Total** with a limit of `4`, then **Normalize All**.
6. Compare the group list with a fresh import of the same part. The numbers must match.

Hold `Alt` when you click a Toolbox button to run it on every selected object.

## Common problems

<Accordions type="multiple">
<Accordion title="The whole mesh explodes into spikes in game" id="mesh-explodes">

The group list has a gap, is out of order, or lost a number. Run the clean-up above, compare the list with a fresh import, and export again.

</Accordion>
<Accordion title="One area stretches when the character moves" id="one-area-stretches">

That area picked up weights from the wrong bone, often where an arm touches the body. Copy that group again with **Transfer Weights**, smooth it, and run **Normalize All**. Lining your mesh up closer to the original helps.

</Accordion>
<Accordion title="Data Transfer changed nothing" id="transfer-did-nothing">

Check these in order:

- **Source** is empty or points to the wrong object.
- Your mesh has no groups with the same names. Click **Generate Data Layers**.
- **Object Transform** is off, so the copy looks in the wrong place.
- You are previewing with an Armature modifier above the Data Transfer modifier. Move Data Transfer to the top.

</Accordion>
<Accordion title="Hair or cloth does not sway" id="no-sway">

The part is weighted to a rigid group such as the head or the spine. Weight it to the original's moving groups instead: the hair, coat tail or ribbon bones of that character. If the part has no moving bones where you need them, you cannot add any.

</Accordion>
<Accordion title="Huge spikes appear right after export" id="spikes-after-export">

The weights are not normalized, or a group name is not a plain number. Run **Normalize All** and check that every group is named `0`, `1`, `2` and so on.

</Accordion>
</Accordions>

More symptoms and fixes are on [Model problems in game](/docs/honkai-star-rail/errors/model-problems).
