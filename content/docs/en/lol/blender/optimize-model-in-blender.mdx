<LevelPill level="intermediate" />

League of Legends models can have more than 65,000 vertices, as long as each submesh stays within the limit. You can have one submesh with 65,000 vertices and another with 65,000 vertices, and the model will still export.

The problem is that too many vertices can lower your FPS. On high-end devices, the difference can be significant, sometimes around 100 FPS. On low-end devices, the change may not be as noticeable.

This guide shows you how to reduce your vertex count so your model has less impact on FPS.

<img
  src="/wiki-images/optimize-model-1.webp"
  alt="A 2B model in Blender with the statistics overlay showing 219,906 vertices"
/>

This 2B model has **219,906 vertices**, more than three times the 65,000 limit for a single submesh.

<Callout type="info" title="Can't see the vertex count?">
  Open the **Overlays** dropdown at the top right of the viewport and enable
  **Statistics**.
</Callout>

## Merge by distance

Most imported models have many duplicate vertices stacked on top of each other. You can merge these vertices without changing how the model looks. This can remove a large number of vertices.

<Steps>
<Step>

### Select the model

1. Select your model.
2. Press `Tab` to enter **Edit Mode**.

<img
  src="/wiki-images/optimize-model-2.webp"
  alt="The model in Edit Mode with the whole mesh selected"
/>

</Step>
<Step>

### Select every vertex

Press `A` to select every vertex.

</Step>
<Step>

### Merge the vertices

1. Press `M` to open the **Merge** menu.
2. Pick **By Distance**.

<Callout type="warning" title="Always use By Distance">
  The other options squash everything you selected into a single point.
</Callout>

<img
  src="/wiki-images/optimize-model-3.webp"
  alt="The Merge menu open with At Center, At Cursor, Collapse and By Distance"
/>

</Step>
</Steps>

Blender shows how many vertices it removed at the bottom of the window. In this example, it removed 188,335 vertices. The model dropped to **31,571 vertices**, well under the limit.

<img
  src="/wiki-images/optimize-model-4.webp"
  alt="After Merge by Distance the model has 31,571 vertices and Blender reports 188,335 removed"
/>

If the count is still too high, raise **Merge Distance** in the panel at the bottom left a little. For example, try `0.001`.

Don't push it too far, or nearby details may start fusing together.

## Decimate

If merging isn't enough, use the **Decimate** modifier to simplify the mesh.

<Steps>
<Step>

### Open the Modifiers tab

1. Press `Tab` to return to **Object Mode**.
2. Select your model.
3. Open the **Modifiers** tab in the Properties panel on the right. Look for the blue wrench icon.

<img
  src="/wiki-images/optimize-model-5.webp"
  alt="The Modifiers tab, a blue wrench icon, circled in the Properties panel"
/>

</Step>
<Step>

### Add the Decimate modifier

1. Click **Add Modifier**.
2. Search for `Decimate`.

</Step>
<Step>

### Set the modifier options

1. Switch the mode to **Planar**.
2. Raise **Angle Limit** little by little.
3. Watch the vertex count until it reaches the amount you want.
4. Enable **UVs** under **Delimit** so the textures don't get stretched.

<img
  src="/wiki-images/optimize-model-6.webp"
  alt="The Decimate modifier set to Planar with Angle Limit and the Delimit options"
/>

</Step>
<Step>

### Apply the modifier

1. Open the dropdown arrow on the modifier.
2. Click **Apply** to bake the change into the mesh before you export.

</Step>
</Steps>

<Callout type="info" title="Combine both">
  On very high-poly models, merge by distance first, then decimate what's left.
  Aim for under 60,000 vertices per submesh to leave some room. For more model
  prep steps, see the [3D Model Swap guide](/docs/lol/blender/3d-model-swap).
</Callout>
