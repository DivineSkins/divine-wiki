<LevelPill level="advanced" />

This guide puts a whole new model on a Genshin Impact character. The character keeps its skeleton, animations and face. Your model replaces everything else. Written for XXMI Tools 1.8.2 on Blender 4.5 LTS, with GIMI 8.9.4.

<Callout type="warning" title="Your own or licensed models only">
  Use a model you made yourself, or one whose license lets you use it in a game
  mod and share it. This guide does not cover porting characters from other
  games, films or anime. Each outfit, paid ones included, is its own model with
  its own hashes: never put a paid outfit over its free version.
</Callout>

## Know the limits first

- **The skeleton stays the game's.** You cannot add bones. Your model moves with the target's animations, so a tall model on a short character still runs like the short one.
- **Size is only visual.** The hitbox and the camera stay the same. Keep your model close to the original's height and build.
- **The parts stay the game's.** You fill the parts the target already has, such as `Head`, `Body`, `Dress` and `Extra`. You cannot add a new part.
- **The face stays the game's.** A normal export does not touch it. You join your neck to it. Changing its shape needs [Edit a face](/docs/genshin-impact/models/faces).
- **Stay near the original vertex count.** Very heavy meshes, with hundreds of thousands of vertices, cost performance.

Pick a target whose body is close to your model in shape and size. A very different body cannot follow the game's bones in a sensible way.

## Prepare your model

1. Import the target's dump first and keep a hidden `Reference` copy, as [Get a model into Blender](/docs/genshin-impact/models/import-into-blender) shows.
2. Import your model with <MenuPath path="File > Import > FBX" />, or glTF. For a VRoid model you made, install the VRM add-on for Blender first.
3. Delete what the game cannot use: physics and dummy bones, extra objects, and shape keys you will not keep.
4. Read your model's license again if you did not make it. Many downloadable models, such as MMD or avatar models, forbid use in other games.

## Match the pose and size

Weight copying and automatic bone matching both compare positions. The closer your model sits on the original, the better they work.

1. Move, rotate and scale your model with `G`, `R` and `S` until it overlaps the imported original. Do not add an extra 90 degree rotation.
2. Select your armature, press `Ctrl+Tab` for Pose Mode, and rotate the arms and legs until they match the original's pose.
3. Make that pose the new rest pose. Apply the **Armature** modifier on your mesh to keep the posed shape, then choose <MenuPath path="Pose > Apply > Apply Pose as Rest Pose" /> on the armature, and add the **Armature** modifier back.
4. Select your mesh and armature, press `Ctrl+A` and choose **All Transforms**.
5. Turn on the **Face Orientation** overlay. Blue faces point out, red faces point in. Select the red ones and flip them with `Alt+N` > **Flip**.

## Map your bones to the game's numbers

On an imported part, each vertex group is named after a game bone: `0`, `1`, `2` and so on. Your model's groups are named after its own bones, like `UpperArm.L`. The game cannot read those names. Every group must get the number of the game bone for the same body area.

| Your model                                  | Method                                                        |
| ------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Has a rig with bones for each body area     | [Rename the bones by hand](#rename-the-bones-by-hand)         |
| Has a rig, and you want a faster first pass | [Match them with ArmatureXXMI](#match-them-with-armaturexxmi) |
| Has no rig, or bones that match nothing     | [Copy weights from the original body](#copy-weights-instead)  |

### Rename the bones by hand

This is slow, but it keeps your model's own good weights.

1. Open the original in a second Blender window, or show the `Reference` copy next to your model.
2. On the original, enter Weight Paint mode and click through its groups. Note which number moves which body area: hips, spine, chest, neck, upper arms, forearms, hands, thighs, knees, feet.
3. Select your armature and press `Tab` for Edit Mode.
4. Click a bone, press `F2`, and type the game number for the same area. Blender renames the matching vertex group too.
5. For a bone the game has no match for, type the number of the bone it should follow. Blender names it like `12.001`. The clean-up below folds it into `12`.
6. Hair, skirt and cape bones go to the target's own hair, skirt and cape groups, if it has them. Otherwise use the nearest body bone.
7. Check that every group now has a number and no group keeps a text name.

Save before you start, and save often. Group numbers differ for every character, so always read them from your own target.

### Match them with ArmatureXXMI

<ToolCard
  name="ArmatureXXMI"
  href="https://github.com/Seris0/Gustav0/tree/main/Addons/ArmatureXXMI"
  badge="Untested"
  icon={<Icon name="GitHub" />}
>
  Blender add-on that compares where each group's weights sit on the original
  and on your model, then renames your bones to the game's numbers. By Gustav0.
  Its author says it is for advanced users.
</ToolCard>

1. Install `RenameArmatureXXMI_V26.py` like any add-on and enable it.
2. Press `N` and open the **XXMI Scripts** tab, then the **ArmatureXXMI** panel.
3. Set the mode to **Merged**. The author recommends it for Genshin Impact.
4. Set the base collection to the imported original, the target collection to your model's meshes, and the armature to your model's armature.
5. Click **Match Armature**. Then click **Clean Armature** to remove bones that no group uses.
6. Check the result by hand: in Weight Paint mode, each number on your model must move the same area as on the original. Left and right must not be swapped.

### Copy weights instead

If your model has no usable rig, delete its vertex groups and copy the weights from the original body with a **Data Transfer** modifier, as [Weight paint a mesh](/docs/genshin-impact/models/weight-painting) shows. That page also covers fixing single areas, posing with a test armature, and the Robust Weight Transfer add-on for clothing.

### Clean up the groups

Run this on every finished part, as on any export: **Merge shared name Vertex Groups**, then **Remove unused Vertex Groups**, then **Fill gaps in Vertex Groups**, with **Normalize weights to format** on at export. Then compare the group list with a fresh import of the same part. The numbers must match.

## Split your model into the game's parts

<img
  src="/wiki-images/genshin-impact-models-replace-a-character-2.webp"
  alt="A table: hair and hats go in Head, body, skin and shoes in Body, cloth that shows its inside in Dress, and a part with nothing left stays as an empty Extra object. One texture set per part. You cannot add a part, add bones or change the face mesh"
/>

1. Open the target's `hash.json` and note its parts and which textures each one uses. `Body` and `Dress` often share one texture.
2. In Edit Mode, select the pieces for one part and press `P` > **Selection**. Repeat for each part.
3. Delete body faces that your clothing hides. They cost performance and poke through when the character moves.
4. Give each part one texture set. Pack several materials into one atlas, as [UV maps, vertex colors, and outlines](/docs/genshin-impact/models/uvs-and-vertex-colors) shows. That page also covers `TEXCOORD1.xy` for cloth with an inside.

## Join your neck to the game's face

The game draws its own face on top of the head. Keep it, and join your body to it at the neck:

1. In the original part that holds the neck, keep the neck and everything above it. Delete the rest of the original mesh in that part.
2. On your model, delete the head above the neck. Keep your hair only if it fits the game's head.
3. Trim both necks so their open edges meet.
4. Move the edges together with `G`, with Proportional Editing on (`O`).
5. Select both edge rings and press `M` > **By Distance** to merge them.

A head modeled into your body mesh instead loses all face expressions.

## Join, texture and export

1. Empty each original part object and join your piece into it with `Ctrl+J`, as [Export your mod from Blender](/docs/genshin-impact/models/export-your-mod) shows. A part with nothing left stays as an empty object.
2. Copy the `COLOR` attribute from the original with Data Transfer. Without it, outlines and shading look wrong.
3. Check that every UV map is named `TEXCOORD.xy`, plus `TEXCOORD1.xy` where the part had one.
4. Export with XXMI Tools.
5. Copy your textures over the exported ones, with the same file names. Save the diffuse as `BC7 (sRGB, DX 11+)` and the light map and normal map as `BC7 (Linear, DX 11+)`.

Three texture rules matter more on a full replacement:

- **Diffuse alpha is the glow mask.** A diffuse with no alpha makes the whole part glow. Paint the alpha dark where nothing should glow.
- **Build the light map from the original.** Pick the original's colors for skin, cloth and metal and paint them on your layout. Wrong values make skin look grey or blue.
- **Fill the normal map flat** where you have no detail, with the original's base color.

[What each texture map does](/docs/genshin-impact/textures/texture-maps) explains each channel.

## Check it in game

1. Copy the mod into your `Mods` folder and turn off other mods for this character.
2. Switch to another character, press `F10`, and switch back.
3. Check the character screen, then run, stop a sprint, attack and use the burst. Some animations stretch parts that look fine when standing.
4. Look closely at the neck, the shoulders, the hips and the knees.
5. Fix what you see in Blender, export again, and press `F10`.

## Common problems

<Accordions type="multiple">
<Accordion title="An arm or leg follows the wrong side" id="left-right-swapped">

Left and right bones got swapped numbers. In Weight Paint mode, compare each limb's groups with the original and rename the wrong ones.

</Accordion>
<Accordion title="Shoulders or hips stretch when the character moves" id="joints-stretch">

The weights near the joint follow the wrong bone, or the pose did not match before you mapped the bones. Copy that area's group again from the original, smooth it, and run **Normalize All**.

</Accordion>
<Accordion title="A gap opens at the neck" id="neck-gap">

The two necks do not share their edge, or their weights differ. Merge the edge rings again and give the seam the same weights as the original neck.

</Accordion>
<Accordion title="The whole model glows, or skin looks grey" id="glow-or-grey">

Glow comes from the diffuse alpha. Grey or blue skin comes from the light map. Fix the texture, not the mesh.

</Accordion>
<Accordion title="Spikes appear when the character first loads" id="first-load-spikes">

The new vertex count is not applied yet on the first draw. Switch to another character and back. If the spikes stay, check the vertex groups for gaps.

</Accordion>
</Accordions>

More symptoms are on [Model problems in game](/docs/genshin-impact/errors/model-problems).
