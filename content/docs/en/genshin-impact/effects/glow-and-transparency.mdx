<LevelPill level="intermediate" />

You will make parts of your own mod glow, turn parts like glass or lace see-through, or give a part its own outline color. All of it runs through TexFx, the glow, transparency and outline library that ships inside GIMI. You need a mod you made yourself, with an `.ini` from the XXMI Tools export. Written for GIMI 8.9.4, which includes TexFx 1.066.

<Callout type="info" title="TexFx is already installed">
  GIMI ships TexFx in `GIMI\Core\GIMI\Libraries\TexFx`, and XXMI Launcher keeps
  it up to date. Never download TexFx, never put it in your `Mods` folder, and
  never ship it inside your mod. If an old guide made you copy TexFx into
  `Mods`, delete that copy: two copies cause errors and crashes.
</Callout>

<Callout type="danger" title="Keep it fair">
  Use glow and transparency only on your own character's parts. Never make
  enemies, walls, fog or effects see-through or easier to spot. That is a mod
  that gives an unfair advantage.
</Callout>

## Two kinds of glow

- **Burst glow.** The game already makes some areas glow when the burst is ready. That glow comes from the alpha of the diffuse texture. To make a new area glow with the burst, give it the same alpha value as an area that already does. [Your first mod: recolor a character](/docs/genshin-impact/textures/recolor-a-character) shows how to reach the alpha.
- **Glow all the time, in any color.** That is TexFx. You paint an extra texture, the effects mask, and point your part at it. The rest of this page covers TexFx.

{/* NEEDS HANDS-ON TEST: that copying the diffuse alpha value of an area that already glows makes a new area glow with the burst on Genshin Impact 7.2 */}

## How TexFx reads your textures

TexFx reads two textures you make yourself, painted on the same UV layout as the part's diffuse:

- the **effects mask** in slot `ps-t69`, for glow, bloom and see-through areas
- the **outline map** in slot `ps-t70`, for your own outline color

<img
  src="/wiki-images/genshin-impact-effects-glow-and-transparency-1.webp"
  alt="The effects mask for slot ps-t69, saved as BC7 Linear: black does nothing, green is glow brightness, blue is bloom, red 1 to 254 is see-through and red 255 is not drawn. The outline map for slot ps-t70, saved as BC7 sRGB: RGB is the outline color, alpha above 50 percent keeps the outline"
/>

The effects mask alpha, values 1 to 63, can also swap colors. This page does not cover that.

## Make a glow or see-through part

<Steps>
<Step>

### Paint the effects mask

1. Open the part's diffuse in Paint.NET. You use it only as a guide for where things are.
2. Add a new layer and fill it with black, `000000`. Black means no effect.
3. Paint the areas you want with one of these colors:

| Paint          | Hex      | Result                                     |
| -------------- | -------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| Bright green   | `00FF00` | Strong glow, all the time                  |
| Green and blue | `00FF80` | Glow with a bloom halo                     |
| Dark red       | `800000` | About half see-through, for glass or veils |
| Pure red       | `FF0000` | Not drawn at all, a clean cut-out          |

4. Use darker values for a softer effect: brightness sets the strength.
5. Delete the diffuse layer, then flatten the image.
6. Save it in your mod folder as `FurinaBodyTexFx.dds`, as `BC7 (Linear, DX 11+)` with **Generate Mip Maps** ticked. Keep the diffuse's width and height.

For gradients, creators build each channel on its own layer. They turn a black and white template into red, green and blue with **Curves**, then set the layers to **Additive** blend:

<img
  src="/wiki-images/genshin-impact-effects-glow-and-transparency-2.webp"
  alt="Paint.NET 5.0.9 with a glow mask split into R, G and B layers over a MASK template, the Curves window with Red and Green ticked, and Layer Properties with Blend Mode set to Additive"
/>

</Step>
<Step>

### Add the mask to your .ini

Add a resource for your mask at the end of the `.ini`:

```ini
[ResourceFurinaBodyTexFx]
filename = FurinaBodyTexFx.dds
```

Then pick how much of the part it covers.

<Tabs items={["The whole part", "One object only"]}>

<Tab value="The whole part">

In the part's `[TextureOverride...]` section, add two lines right after `run = CommandList\GIMI\SetTextures`:

```ini
ps-t69 = ResourceFurinaBodyTexFx
run = CommandList\TexFx\Transparency
```

The TexFx line always goes after the ORFix, NNFix or `SetTextures` line.

</Tab>

<Tab value="One object only">

Use this when only one object of a part should change, like a pair of glasses inside `Body`. The export writes one `drawindexed` line per Blender object, with the object's name above it:

```ini
; FurinaBodyGlasses (934)
drawindexed = 1674, 32985, 0
```

Replace that `drawindexed` line with these four lines:

```ini
ps-t69 = ResourceFurinaBodyTexFx
$\TexFx\_1 = 1674
$\TexFx\_2 = 32985
run = CommandList\TexFx\Component
```

`$\TexFx\_1` takes the first number of the old `drawindexed` line, and `$\TexFx\_2` the second. Delete the old `drawindexed` line. Before the `drawindexed` lines of objects without TexFx, add `ps-t69 = null`. To put the effect on a key, wrap these lines in a toggle, as in [Add a toggle](/docs/genshin-impact/ini/add-a-toggle).

</Tab>

</Tabs>

Older guides write `Transparency.1` or `Transparency.0`. You do not need the number any more. `CommandList\TexFx\T` and `CommandList\TexFx\C` are short names for the same commands.

{/* NEEDS HANDS-ON TEST: CommandList\TexFx\Component with $\TexFx\_1 and $\TexFx\_2 on an XXMI Tools export in Genshin Impact 7.2, and that the commands work without the .1 and .0 endings */}

</Step>
<Step>

### Test it

1. Switch to another character and press `F10`.
2. Switch back and check the glow in daylight, at night and indoors.
3. Hold `F9` to compare with the original.

</Step>
</Steps>

### Cut-outs only

For lace, leaves or a ragged hem, a mask with only black and pure red `FF0000` is enough. Add the `ps-t69 = ...` line and **no** TexFx command. With the command the edges still work, but they look pixelated.

## Set your own outline color

1. Copy the part's diffuse and darken it, or paint the outline colors you want on the same layout.
2. Keep the alpha opaque where you want outlines. Alpha below 50% removes the outline there.
3. Save it as `FurinaBodyOutline.dds`, as `BC7 (sRGB, DX 11+)` with mipmaps.
4. Add `ps-t70 = ResourceFurinaBodyOutline` to the part's section, and a `[ResourceFurinaBodyOutline]` with its `filename =`.

An outline map alone needs no TexFx command.

## Common problems

<Accordions type="multiple">
<Accordion title="Outlines turned blue after an update" id="blue-outlines">

This was a TexFx bug in 2026, fixed in TexFx 1.066. Update GIMI through XXMI Launcher. Keep `ps-t70`. Old guides tell you to change it to `ps-t10` or `ps-t71`, which only turns your custom outline off.

</Accordion>
<Accordion title="The whole part glows or goes see-through" id="whole-part">

The mask has color where it should be black. Check that the background is pure black, `000000`, and that you saved it as Linear, not sRGB.

</Accordion>
<Accordion title="Wrong textures or effects on other parts" id="wrong-parts">

Add `ps-t69 = null` to the parts and objects that do not use TexFx, before their `drawindexed` lines.

Still wrong? The TexFx README suggests the old endings as a fallback: try `CommandList\TexFx\Transparency.1` on a part with a normal map, or `.0` on a part without one.

</Accordion>
<Accordion title="Nothing changes" id="nothing-changes">

Check that `filename =` matches your mask's name and that the `ps-t69` line sits after `SetTextures`. Read the yellow text after `F10`: [Test and reload your mod](/docs/genshin-impact/basics/test-your-mod) explains GIMI's warnings.

</Accordion>
</Accordions>
